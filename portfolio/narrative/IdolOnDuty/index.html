Conceptualization


Concept art created by Emi Han.

Idol On Duty was conceptualized by Emi Han, our team's character artist. They were the one who formed Backstage Crew with the concept of a rhythm beat-em-up where you play as a half j-pop magical girl, half metal-playing demon.

I was brought on early in the team formation process as the narrative designer. My role was to integrate and maintain the context for gameplay.

One of my first contributions to the project was establishing the driving motivation of the player character. I immediately thought of what would be an emotional hook for the game that would make sense for a burgeoning J-Pop idol. A theme of living your own life and not the one others want for you emerged, the relationship between a mother and daughter at the center.


Concept Art by Emi Han

To fit the light-hearted tone our team was going for, I thought of cheesy 90s film plots about greedy companies buying a forest to destroy it and build a mall. I exaggerated that concept even further by destroying all of music in existence as the target just to get closer to her daughter.

Rebellious magical girl beats up her CEO mother's underpaid businessman lackeys is the compelling hook that properly set up the scaffolding for gameplay.

Cutscenes

I advocated for a cutscene system, working with programmers Kishan Patel and Andres Trujillo to integrate the dialogue system with Unreal Engine's sequencer. I storyboarded and wrote two cutscenes.

When drafting the cutscenes, I understood that we did not have the resources for custom animations. Because of that, I focused on eliciting emotion with limited resources.

Pre-Boss Cutscene

When drafting characters within a story, especially within a comedic framework, I consider what their punchline will be. RoBort is an example of that line of thought.

When conceptualizing how the player would learn the controls and initial context, I imagined Aiko's inner dialogue. But through a series of events, most importantly the creation of a interactive NPC by our producer Andres. With limited art to draw from, I used the "Stanamite" model and created the character of RoBort.

RoBort's optimism and his support of Aiko and Manami made him a perfect victim of Manami's cruelty, heightening the stakes before the final battle.

Final Cutscene

For the final cutscene, it was very important that the main conflict of the narrative be resolved, that being Aiko's desire to become a idol against her mother's desire to control her.

I did not have access to facial animation, and Manami was confined to images on a screen due to more art constraints. Even considering all of that, I still wanted to reveal the emotional core of Aiko and Manami's relationship. Manami's desires to clip Aiko's wings because she was unable to become a star herself, hiding her feelings under a defense of family honor and professionalism.

Trailer

I edited and directed the trailer for the Steam release. I storyboarded two animated sequences, animated by Elijah Taimanglo and Emi Han in that order.

As a trailer, it's the first time someone is exposed to the narrative, so it was important that I get the core elements across.

First animated section establishes Aiko, her mother, and the enemies the player will be facing.

Second animated section reflects Aiko's duality and her desire to "reach for the stars."
