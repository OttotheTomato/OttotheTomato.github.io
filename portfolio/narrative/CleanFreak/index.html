Clean Freak

Clean Freak is a Twine project made for my Narrative Design class. It started life as a short story treatment that I then developed into a Twine prototype.

The main purpose of this prototype was to demonstrate I could implement a affinity system.

The two non-player characters, Andy and Gerald, each have an Empathy value and a Respect value.

By empathizing with a character, such as defending them in an argument, the player gains an Empathy point with that character. If the player gains a character's respect, such as following their plan, they gain a Respect point with that character.

By splitting affinity to multiple values, it allows for more nuance for character dialogue. A character may empathize with the player character but not respect their decisions and vice versa.

The prototype story structure was overly complicated and overscoped because I believed that it was supposed to encompass the entire story. For the final submission, I simplified the routes to have four endings, two for each NPC depending on their empathy and respect.

Problem

The final submission was rough for multiple reasons.

  • Grammatical mistakes.

    • There was plentiful grammatical mistakes due to a rushed editing process and lack of autocorrect tools in Twine.

  • Lack of clear character development.

    • In the feedback I recieved, it was noted that there was no clear character development for any of the characters.

  • Lack of clear influence on the events of the story.

    • From the feedback, "although I can see the immediate consequences of my choices, I don't have a very strong sense of how I'm impacting the story overall."

    The system is clear in its effect, but the story does not reflect this effect.

    As mentioned in the feedback, "the last passages are structured well and have good variable checks, but the options presented didn't give me a strong sense that the action which plays out was based on my earlier choices."

Solution

If I wanted to use this project as a portfolio piece, I needed to revise it.

To revise it I had to implement:

  • An editing pass.

    • I was especially careful to check the grammar and punctuation to make sure the writing was polished.

  • Further characterization through dialogue and action.

    • Instead of describing actions in a way that could apply to any character, I edited and added sections to the text that reflect a character's traits.

    The same goes for dialogue, of which I wanted to bring out Andy and Gerald's specific voices.

  • More ripples that demonstrate the player's effect on the characters.

    • In the original submission the effect of the player's choices were only seen near the end of the game. In my revisions I focused on making sure that every scene after the first choice would be influenced by the player's actions. It was also important that said changes be more than just a single swapped line of dialogue or two, as that isn't enough to illustrate how the player has effected the characters.

Result

To demonstrate that my revisions had improved the elements that were lacking in the original submission I conducted playtesting.
